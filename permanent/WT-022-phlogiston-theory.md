# WT-022: Phlogiston Theory (燃素说)

## 🏛️ 技术档案

- **技术名称**: Phlogiston Theory / 燃素说 (rán sù shuō)
- **技术领域**: Chemical Theory / Scientific Paradigm / Combustion Chemistry
- **活跃时期**: 1667 (proposed) → 1780s (overthrown)
- **鼎盛时期**: 1730s-1770s (widely accepted in Europe)
- **衰退时间**: 1770s-1780s (Lavoisier's oxygen theory)
- **核心特征**: Fire as a material substance contained in combustible materials
- **关键人物**: Johann Joachim Becher (1635-1682), Georg Ernst Stahl (1660-1734), Antoine Lavoisier (1743-1794)

---

## 📜 Brief History

**燃烧本质的世纪误解。**
Becher proposed "terra pinguis" (fatty earth) 1667; Stahl developed phlogiston theory 1703; dominated European chemistry for century; Lavoisier's oxygen experiments 1770s demonstrated combustion requires oxygen, not releases phlogiston; theory collapsed by 1790s.

---

## ⚰️ 衰退原因

### 1. **Experimental Contradictions (1770s-1780s)**
   - **Mass gain problem**: Metals gain mass when burned (phlogiston should have negative mass)
   - **Oxygen discovery**: Priestley (1774) isolated "dephlogisticated air" (oxygen)
   - **Lavoisier's experiments**: Quantitative measurements showed mass conservation
   - **Hydrogen combustion**: Cavendish (1766) produced water from "inflammable air"
   - **Animal respiration**: Similar to combustion, consumes "dephlogisticated air"

### 2. **Theoretical Inconsistencies**
   - **Negative mass problem**: Phlogiston must have negative mass to explain metal calcination
   - **Circular reasoning**: Phlogiston defined by combustion, combustion explained by phlogiston
   - **Infinite flexibility**: Theory could be adjusted to explain any result
   - **Unfalsifiability**: No experiment could definitively disprove theory
   - **Substance undetectable**: Phlogiston never isolated or observed directly

### 3. **Paradigm Shift Requirements**
   - **Quantitative chemistry**: Lavoisier introduced precision measurement
   - **Element concept**: Modern definition of chemical elements needed
   - **Conservation of mass**: Fundamental principle phlogiston violated
   - **Oxygen theory**: Simpler, more consistent explanation
   - **Nomenclature reform**: New systematic naming required new theoretical framework

### 4. **Social and Generational Factors**
   - **Scientific generation change**: Older chemists resistant, younger adopted oxygen theory
   - **National differences**: French (Lavoisier) vs English (Priestley) acceptance rates
   - **Institutional support**: French Academy supported new paradigm
   - **Textbook revision**: New educational materials taught oxygen theory
   - **Death of defenders**: Stahl, defenders died, new generation embraced Lavoisier

### 5. **Methodological Revolution**
   - **Balance precision**: Improved balances enabled accurate mass measurements
   - **Quantitative over qualitative**: Mass of reactants/products more important than qualities
   - **Gas collection techniques**: Pneumatic chemistry enabled systematic gas study
   - **Controlled experiments**: Isolation of variables, reproducibility emphasis
   - **Mathematical formulation**: Stoichiometry required consistent theoretical basis

### 6. **Explanatory Power Deficit**
   - **Combustion**: Oxygen theory explained both burning and rusting
   - **Respiration**: Animal breathing explained as oxygen consumption
   - **Acid formation**: Non-metal oxides forming acids understood
   - **Calculus of reactions**: Quantitative predictions possible with oxygen theory
   - **Unification**: Single principle explained diverse phenomena

| Aspect | Phlogiston Theory | Oxygen Theory |
|--------|-------------------|---------------|
| **Combustion** | Releases phlogiston into air | Combines with oxygen |
| **Metal calcination** | Loses phlogiston | Gains oxygen |
| **Mass change** | Decreases (phlogiston lost) | Increases (oxygen gained) |
| **Air explanation** | Becomes saturated with phlogiston | Oxygen consumed |

## 🔬 Technical Deep Dive

### Theory Structure

#### **Core Postulates**
```
Phlogiston Theory Principles:

1. All combustible materials contain phlogiston
   - Wood, coal, sulfur, phosphorus rich in phlogiston
   - Metals contain moderate phlogiston
   - Ash, rust (calces) phlogiston-free

2. Combustion = phlogiston release
   - Burning material releases phlogiston to air
   - Flame = escaping phlogiston visible
   - Heat = phlogiston release process

3. Air has limited phlogiston capacity
   - Air becomes "phlogisticated" (saturated)
   - Combustion stops in phlogisticated air
   - Fresh air needed for continued burning

4. Calcination = phlogiston loss
   - Metals heated lose phlogiston
   - Resulting calx (oxide) = metal minus phlogiston
   - Metal can be restored by adding phlogiston (charcoal)

5. Respiration = phlogiston release
   - Animals release phlogiston when breathing
   - Closed spaces become phlogisticated
   - Fresh air needed to sustain life
```

#### **Theoretical Adjustments**
```
Explaining Mass Gain (Metal Calcination):

Problem: Metals gain mass when burned
Phlogiston theory predicts: Mass should decrease

Solution 1 (Early): Phlogiston has negative mass
- Fire rises, so phlogiston possesses levity (negative gravity)
- Negative phlogiston = positive mass gain

Solution 2 (Later): Phlogiston = principle of fire, not material
- Phlogiston immaterial, doesn't contribute to mass
- Loss of phlogiston allows combination with "earths" in air
- Earths add mass

Problem: No independent evidence for negative mass
Ad hoc adjustment designed solely to save theory
```

### Key Experiments and Interpretations

#### **Combustion in Closed Vessels**
```
Experiment (Robert Boyle, 1663):
- Metal sealed in container with air
- Heated until calcined
- Container mass unchanged
- Metal + container before = Metal + container after

Phlogiston interpretation:
- Phlogiston released from metal
- Phlogiston absorbed by air in container
- Total mass conserved within system

Modern interpretation:
- Metal combines with oxygen in air
- No mass change (sealed system)
- Demonstrates conservation of mass

Critical issue: Phlogiston theorists didn't weigh air separately
Lavoisier's innovation: Weighed air before and after
```

#### **Restoration of Metals**
```
Experiment:
- Calx (metal oxide) heated with charcoal
- Pure metal produced

Phlogiston interpretation:
- Charcoal (rich in phlogiston) donates phlogiston to calx
- Calx + phlogiston = metal restored

Modern interpretation:
- Carbon (charcoal) removes oxygen from metal oxide
- Metal oxide + carbon → Metal + carbon dioxide
- Reduction-oxidation (redox) reaction

Both explanations predict observed result
Critical difference: Mass balance calculations
```

#### **Pneumatic Chemistry Discoveries**
```
Joseph Priestley (1770s):
- Heated red mercuric oxide
- Produced gas supporting combustion better than air
- Called it "dephlogisticated air" (air without phlogiston)

Phlogiston interpretation:
- Heating removed phlogiston from mercury
- Result = pure air (all phlogiston removed)
- "Dephlogisticated air" = air before combustion polluted it

Modern interpretation:
- Mercury oxide → Mercury + Oxygen
- Oxygen supports combustion
- Air = ~21% oxygen + nitrogen + other gases

Priestley's phlogiston commitment:
- Discovered oxygen but interpreted through phlogiston lens
- Shows how theory determines observation
```

### Historical Development

#### **Becher's Terra Pinguis (1667)**
```
Johann Joachim Becher's theory:
- Three types of earth:
  1. Terra lapidea (stony earth) - fusibility
  2. Terra fluida (fluid earth) - fluidity/volatility
  3. Terra pinguis (fatty earth) - combustibility

Terra pinguis:
- "Fatty" principle in combustible materials
- Released during combustion
- Similar to phlogiston (precursor concept)
- Not fully developed theory

Influence on Stahl:
- Becher's student, expanded terra pinguis concept
- Developed into full phlogiston theory
- Emphasized phlogiston as material substance
```

#### **Stahl's Phlogiston Theory (1703)**
```
Georg Ernst Stahl's formulation:
- Unified theory of combustion, calcination, respiration
- Phlogiston as material principle of fire
- Explained diverse phenomena with single principle
- Widely accepted across Europe

Key contributions:
- Clear, comprehensive theory
- Explained why combustion stops (air saturation)
- Connected burning, rusting, breathing
- Provided framework for chemical research

Legacy:
- Dominated chemistry for 80+ years
- Enabled systematic research program
- Eventually overthrown by Lavoisier
```

#### **Lavoisier's Oxygen Theory (1770s-1780s)**
```
Antoine Lavoisier's experiments:
1. Quantitative measurements (precision balances)
2. Mass conservation in closed systems
3. Identification of gases (oxygen, nitrogen, hydrogen)
4. Systematic nomenclature

Key experiments:
- Phosphorus/sulfur combustion: Mass gain, not loss
- Mercury calcination: Mass gain from air
- Reduction of calces: Charcoal removes oxygen
- Water synthesis: Hydrogen + oxygen → water

Theory rejection:
- 1780s: "Anti-phlogiston" paper
- 1789: Traité Élémentaire de Chimie (definitive statement)
- 1790s: Phlogiston theory largely abandoned

Lavoisier's execution (1794):
- "The Republic has no need of savants"
- Delayed some acceptance (French vs English chemistry)
```

### Scientific Resistance and Acceptance

#### **Priestley's Defense**
```
Joseph Priestley (1733-1804):
- Discovered oxygen (1774)
- Lifelong phlogiston defender
- Religious: Phlogiston represented God's design
- Isolated from continental chemistry (English)

Position:
- Phlogiston theory simpler (for him)
- Oxygen theory unnecessarily complex
- No experimental contradiction to phlogiston
- Theory choice matter of philosophical preference

Irony:
- Performed experiments that undermined phlogiston
- Interpreted results within phlogiston framework
- Shows theory-laden observation
```

#### **Cavendish's Ambivalence**
```
Henry Cavendish (1731-1810):
- Discovered hydrogen ("inflammable air")
- Performed water synthesis experiment
- Agreed oxygen theory explained combustion
- Reluctantly abandoned phlogiston

Position:
- Phlogiston as useful heuristic
- Oxygen theory as better description
- Pragmatic acceptance over conviction

Transition:
- Shows generational shift
- Older scientists: intellectual investment in phlogiston
- Younger scientists: adopted oxygen theory more readily
```

#### **Lavoisier's Institutional Advantage**
```
French Academy of Sciences:
- Lavoisier's influential position
- Government support for chemical research
- Centralized scientific community

Advantages:
- Control over publications (Academy mémoires)
- Teaching positions (spread oxygen theory)
- Laboratory resources (systematic experimentation)
- International correspondence (French as scientific language)

English resistance:
- Priestley, Cavendish isolated
- No central authority enforcing paradigm
- Religious/political factors (Priestley's Unitarianism)
- National pride (French vs English science)

Lesson:
- Paradigm changes involve social, institutional factors
- Not purely based on evidence
```

## 📖 Lessons from Phlogiston Theory

### **关于科学进步**

1. **Science as Provisional, Not Absolute**
   - Phlogiston seemed reasonable for 80+ years
   - Current theories may also be wrong
   - Scientific knowledge always tentative
   - Lesson: Intellectual humility about current paradigms

2. **Paradigm Shifts Not Gradual**
   - Not accumulation of facts but radical restructuring
   - Old concepts abandoned, new ones adopted
   - Incommensurability: old vs new paradigms
   - Lesson: Revolutionary change possible in science

3. **Theory-Laden Observation**
   - Same experiments interpreted differently
   - Priestley discovered oxygen but saw dephlogisticated air
   - What you see depends on what you believe
   - Lesson: Objectivity constrained by theoretical framework

### **关于证据与推理**

4. **Underdetermination of Theory by Evidence**
   - Multiple theories explain same evidence
   - Phlogiston and oxygen both explained combustion
   - Evidence alone doesn't force theory choice
   - Lesson: Additional criteria (simplicity, consistency) needed

5. **Falsifiability and Ad Hoc Adjustments**
   - Phlogiston theory constantly adjusted
   - Negative mass, immaterial phlogiston added
   - Lost ability to make specific predictions
   - Lesson: Theories must be testable, falsifiable

6. **Quantitative Revolution**
   - Precise measurements revealed phlogiston problems
   - Balance accuracy: ±0.1 gram vs ±1 gram
   - Mass conservation violated by phlogiston
   - Lesson: Better instruments drive theory change

### **关于社会与心理**

7. **Resistance to Theory Change**
   - Older scientists resistant to oxygen theory
   - Investment in existing theories (intellectual, professional)
   - Generational shift: old dies, young adopts
   - Lesson: Personal factors influence scientific acceptance

8. **Social Factors in Science**
   - Lavoisier's institutional advantages
   - National rivalries (French vs English)
   - Religious beliefs (Priestley's Unitarianism)
   - Lesson: Science not purely rational, involves social factors

9. **Conceptual Metaphors and Language**
   - "Dephlogisticated air" encoded phlogiston assumption
   - Language shapes thought, constrains possibilities
   - Nomenclature reform necessary for paradigm shift
   - Lesson: Pay attention to language in science

### **关于现代科学**

10. **Parallels with Modern Controversies**
    - String theory: Untestable, unfalsifiable?
    - Dark matter: Postulated unobserved substance
    - Multiverse: Infinite flexibility problem?
    - Lesson: Extraordinary claims require extraordinary evidence

11. **Scientific Realism Question**
    - Phlogiston didn't exist despite explaining phenomena
    - Are electrons, quarks, strings real?
    - Theories as instruments vs descriptions of reality
    - Lesson: Distinguish empirical success from ontological commitment

12. **Importance of Historical Perspective**
    - Understanding science's fallible past
    - Avoiding presentist bias (judging past by current standards)
    - Appreciating scientific progress
    - Lesson: Historical context essential for understanding science
