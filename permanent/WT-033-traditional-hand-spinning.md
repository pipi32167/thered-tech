# WT-033: Traditional Hand Spinning (传统手工纺纱)

## 🏛️ 技术档案

- **技术名称**: Traditional Hand Spinning / 传统手工纺纱 (chuán tǒng shǒu gōng fǎng shā)
- **技术领域**: Textile Crafts / Fiber Technology / Domestic Production
- **活跃时期**: ~20,000 BCE (first spun fibers) → 19th century
- **鼎盛时期**: Pre-industrial era (before 1760s spinning jenny)
- **衰退时间**: Late 18th-19th century (spinning jenny, water frame, cotton mills)
- **核心特征**: Twist fibers into thread using drop spindle or spinning wheel
- **关键工具**: Drop spindle, spinning wheel, distaff

---

## 📜 Brief History

**纤维捻线两万年。**
Hand spinning began 20,000 BCE; spinning wheel invented in India (500-800 CE), spread to Europe 13th century; domestic craft produced all thread, yarn; spinning jenny (1764), water frame (1769) industrialized spinning; hand spinning became domestic craft, artistic pursuit; still practiced by artisans, enthusiasts.

## ⚰️ 衰退原因

### 1. **Mechanization: Spinning Jenny (1764)**
   - **Multiple spindles**: 8 spindles at once vs one
   - **Speed**: 8x faster than hand spinning
   - **Cost**: Less expensive per thread
   - **Scale**: Industrial production possible
   - **Obsolescence**: Hand spinning uneconomic

### 2. **Water Frame and Cotton Mills (1769)**
   - **Roller drafting**: Mechanical system replaced hand drafting
   - **Stronger yarn**: Consistent quality
   - **Factory system**: Centralized production
   - **Economies of scale**: Mass production advantages
   - **Cost reduction**: Hand spinning couldn't compete

### 3. **Cotton Gin (1793) and Raw Material**
   - **Cotton boom: Cheap raw material increased demand
   - **Short staple cotton: Difficult to spin by hand
   - **Volume needed: Industrial scale required
   - **Machine spinning: Hand too slow for industrial demand

### 4. **Urbanization and Social Changes**
   - **Domestic production moved to factories**
   - **Women's work changed: Textile production became industrial
   - **Time pressure: Industrial society faster pace
   - **Ready-made yarns: Available commercially
   - **Loss of skill: Fewer people learned hand spinning

### 5. **Economic Factors**
   - **Labor costs**: Hand spinning time-intensive
   - **Price competition: Machine yarn cheaper
   - **Quality consistency: Industrial yarn uniform
   - **Market preference: Standardized yarn demanded
   - **Handmade niche: Luxury, specialty markets only

### 6. **Technical Limitations**
   - **Speed**: One thread at a time (wheel) or very slow (spindle)
   - **Physical effort: Constant hand/foot coordination
   - **Time investment: Hours for small amount
   - **Skill requirement**: Years of practice for quality yarn
   - **Variability: Inconsistent thickness, quality

## 🔬 Technical Deep Dive

### Spinning Tools

#### **Drop Spindle**
```
Basic tool:
- Spindle: Straight stick (8-12 inches)
- Whorl: Weighted disc (center or bottom)
- Fiber supply: Hand-held supply

Operation:
1. Attach fiber: Leader thread tied to spindle
2. Drop spindle: Let gravity spin it
3. Draft fibers: Pulling back while twisting
4. Wind on: Accumulating spun yarn on spindle
5. Repeat: Continuous process

Advantages:
- Portable: Can spin anywhere
- Simple: Minimal equipment
- Ancient: Oldest spinning technology (20,000 years)
- Versatile: Any fiber, any thickness

Disadvantages:
- Slow: Very time-consuming
- Limited yarn: Winding on spindle limits amount
- Skill: Requires practice, coordination

Historical significance:
- Universal: Used worldwide
- Ancient: Predates spinning wheel by millennia
- Continuity: Still used in many cultures
```

#### **Spinning Wheel**
```
Types:
- Great wheel: Large wheel (40-60 inches), hand-turned
- Saxony wheel: Treadle-operated, flywheel (15th century)
- Castle wheel: Double-drive, three-speed (18th century)
- Norwegian wheel: Flywheel, double-drive (18th century)

Components:
- Wheel: Large wheel (drives spindle)
- Drive band: Connects wheel to spindle
- Maidens: Uprights holding flyer/bobbin
- Flyer: Distributes twist evenly
- Bobbin: Stores spun yarn
- Treadle: Foot pedal (Saxony, castle wheels)

Operation (Saxony wheel):
1. Treadle movement: Operates flywheel
2. Drive belt: Transfers motion to spindle
3. Fiber feeding: Hand feeds fiber onto flyer
4. Drafting: Flyer draws out fibers
5. Twisting: Spindle inserts twist
6. Winding: Yarn winds onto bobbin

Advantages over spindle:
- Speed: Much faster spinning
- Quantity: More yarn before winding off
- Consistency: More uniform yarn
- Ergonomics: Sit while spinning

Disadvantages:
- Cost: Expensive equipment
- Portability: Not portable
- Complexity: More parts to maintain
- Space: Requires dedicated space

Historical development:
- India: Invented 500-800 CE
- Spread: Middle East to Europe (13th century)
- Peak: 18th century (before industrialization)
```

#### **Fiber Preparation**
```
Carding:
- Tool: Hand cards (flat paddles with card cloth)
- Purpose: Align fibers, remove debris
- Process: Pull cards apart repeatedly
- Result: Rolag (roll of carded fiber)
- Importance: Critical for even yarn

Combing:
- Tool: Wool combs (multiple tines)
- Purpose: Align long fibers, remove short fibers
- Process: Pull combs through fibers
- Result: Combed top (aligned fibers)
- Use: Worsted yarn (smooth, strong)

Drafting:
- Technique: Pulling back while twisting
- Purpose: Drawing out fibers to uniform thickness
- Skill: Requires practice, judgment
- Critical: Determines yarn quality
- Challenge: Maintaining consistency

Twist:
- Direction: Usually Z-twist (clockwise)
- Amount: Determines yarn strength
- Insertion: Spindle or wheel inserts twist
- Set: Twist sets in yarn (important for knitting)
- Measurement: Twists per inch (tpi)
```

### Fiber Types

#### **Wool**
```
Characteristics:
- Crimp: Natural waviness (elasticity)
- Scales: Overlapping scales (felting ability)
- Length: 2-6 inches (varies by breed)
- Elasticity: Stretches, returns to shape
- Warmth: Insulating properties

Preparation:
- Washing: Remove grease, dirt
- Carding: Align fibers for woolen yarn
- Combing: Align fibers for worsted yarn
- Spinning: Woolen (short draw) or worsted (long draw)

Properties:
- Warm: Best insulator
- Lightweight: Lighter than equivalent cotton
- Elastic: Stretches, returns
- Absorbent: Wicks moisture
- Felting: Scales allow felting

Breed characteristics:
- Merino: Fine, soft, short (2-3 inches)
- Lincoln: Long, strong, lustrous (6-12 inches)
- Romney: Medium length, strong (4-6 inches)
- Shetland: Very fine, soft (2-4 inches)
```

#### **Flax (for Linen)**
```
Characteristics:
- Length: 12-30 inches (very long)
- Stiff: Fibers straight, stiff
- Smooth: Cool, lustrous
- Strong: Very strong fibers
- Inelastic: Does not stretch

Preparation:
- Retting: Rotting stalks to separate fibers
- Breaking: Crushing woody parts
- Scutching: Removing woody shives
- Hackling: Combing fibers to alignment
- Spinning: Requires careful, even spinning

Properties:
- Cool: Cool to touch
- Smooth: Smooth surface
- Strong: Very strong
- Inelastic: No stretch
- Stiff: Can be wiry
- Wrinkles: Wrinkles easily

Linen characteristics:
- Drape: Stiff, crisp drape
- Strength: Durable fabric
- Absorbency: Less absorbent than cotton
- Sheen: Subtle luster
- Historically: Ancient fabric (oldest woven fabric)
```

#### **Cotton**
```
Characteristics:
- Length: 0.5-1.5 inches (short-medium)
- Soft: Soft, comfortable
- Absorbent: Excellent wicking
- Inelastic: Little stretch
- Strong: Moderate strength

Preparation:
- Ginning: Removing seeds
- Carding: Aligning fibers
- Spinning: Relatively easy
- Plying: Often plied for strength

Properties:
- Breathable: Cool in hot weather
- Absorbent: Wicks moisture
- Soft: Soft hand
- Durable: Moderately durable
- Washes well: Easy to clean

Historical context:
- Ancient: Domesticated in India, Peru
- 18th century: Cotton gin enabled industrialization
- Hand spinning: Cotton traditionally handspun in India
- Industrial: First industrialized textile (cotton)
```

### Spinning Techniques

#### **Woolen Spinning (Short Draw)**
```
Technique:
- Draft: Short, quick pulls (1-2 inches)
- Twist: High twist inserted immediately
- Fiber alignment: Air trapped between fibers
- Yarn characteristics: Woolly, warm, lightweight

Purpose:
- Warmth: Trapped air insulates
- Softness: Soft, woolly yarn
- Knitting: Suitable for warm garments

Process:
1. Carded rolag: Hand carded wool
2. Hold lightly: Loose fiber supply
3. Short draw: Quick pulls, high twist
4. High twist: Lots of twist (wraps fiber)
5. Woolen yarn: Air between fibers

Advantages:
- Fast: Easier, quicker than worsted
- Warm: Air trapped for insulation
- Soft: Soft, woolly fabric
- Uses: Excellent for winter garments

Disadvantages:
- Strength: Not as strong as worsted
- Pilling: Tends to pill
- Durability: Less durable
```

#### **Worsted Spinning (Long Draw)**
```
Technique:
- Draft: Long, smooth draw (6+ inches)
- Twist: Lower twist inserted slowly
- Fiber alignment: Fibers parallel
- Yarn characteristics: Smooth, strong, lustrous

Purpose:
- Strength: Strong yarn
- Smoothness: Smooth surface
- Drape: Good drape, crisp
- Durability: Very durable fabric

Process:
1. Combed top: Combed fibers (aligned)
2. Hold firmly: Controlled fiber supply
3. Long draw: Smooth, even draw
4. Controlled twist: Moderate twist
5. Worsted yarn: Parallel fibers

Advantages:
- Strength: Very strong yarn
- Smooth: Smooth yarn surface
- Durability: Long-wearing
- Luster: Yarn luster

Disadvantages:
- Skill: Requires skill, practice
- Time: Slower than woolen spinning
- Fiber length: Needs long fibers
```
